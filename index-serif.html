Triangle Splatting SLAM

A dense RGB-D SLAM system using differentiable triangles as the 3D map representation, with on-the-fly Restricted Delaunay mesh extraction.

Triangle Splatting SLAM uses triangles as the underlying scene representation to enable photorealistic rendering, high-fidelity geometry reconstruction, accurate camera pose estimation, and on-the-fly mesh generation.

Abstract

We present a dense RGB-D SLAM system using differentiable triangles as the 3D map representation. The map can be converted into a connected mesh on-the-fly via Restricted Delaunay triangulation, enabling new online capabilities such as mesh deformation and collisions.

Method

The system uses a differentiable triangle rasteriser that drives both tracking and mapping. Triangles are spawned from back-projected depth maps, optimised photometrically, and converted to a connected mesh on demand.

▲ 01

Tracking

Camera pose minimises a joint photometric (L1 + D-SSIM) and depth loss, with analytic SE(3) Jacobians evaluated in a CUDA backward pass.

▲ 02

Keyframing

New keyframes are selected by triangle co-visibility (IoU vs. the previous keyframe), populating a buffer used during mapping.

▲ 03

Mapping

Joint optimisation of poses, vertices, colour and opacity under photometric, depth, normal and equilateral regularisation losses.

▲ 04

Delaunay Mesh

Restricted Delaunay triangulation converts the soup into a connected mesh online. The method does not use marching cubes, or offline post-processing.

Key design choices

  • Vertex-shared parameterisation. Colour and opacity live on vertices and are interpolated via barycentric coordinates, so changes to connectivity do not introduce discontinuities in the optimisation.
  • Equilateral regulariser. A loss that penalises deviation of internal angles from 60° prevents the formation of degenerate slivers that hurt tracking stability.
  • Analytic camera Jacobians. Pose gradients are derived on the SE(3) manifold and evaluated directly in CUDA, avoiding autodiff overhead during the ~80 tracking iterations per frame.

System overview. Tracking estimates the pose of each incoming RGB-D frame; keyframing selects high-co-visibility frames; mapping jointly refines poses and the triangle soup; Delaunay recovers a connected mesh on demand.
